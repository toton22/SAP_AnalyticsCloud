What "one activity" actually means for you

The whole blueprint rides on a single business scenario. You model, you build, you plan, and you explain your reasoning as you go. Early decisions cascade — pick the wrong model type at the start and every later step inherits the problem. Which is why recall-style cramming does nothing here, and why the practice questions on page 5 finish with fourteen scenarios rather than more trivia.

The rehearsal that actually predicts the outcome

Take a raw CSV. Model it, build a story, add a budget version, run a data action, then explain every choice aloud to a colleague who will interrupt you. If you can do that without reaching for notes, you are ready. If you can only recognise the right answer when you see it written down, you are not — and that gap is exactly what this format was redesigned to expose.

Page two

How SAP Analytics Cloud actually fits together

SAC is one product doing three jobs on one data layer: business intelligence, enterprise planning, and predictive. Almost every exam scenario is a question about where something sits in this chain.

The spine: source to story

Follow one number from a source system to a chart. Every concept you have to learn hangs off a stage in this chain, and the branch you choose at stage two constrains everything after it.

The one decision everything hinges on

If you only truly understand one thing before the exam, make it this. Scenario prompts almost always hide a live-versus-import decision inside a business constraint.

The vocabulary, by branch

Six branches cover essentially everything the exam can ask about. Highlighted terms are the ones that carry the most weight — if a term here means nothing to you, that is your revision list.

Area by area

1

Navigating and getting started

Orientation. Light on marks, but everything else assumes it.

Concepts to know cold
DatasetA prepared table of data. Flexible and quick, but it has no strict dimension typing and cannot be used for planning.
ModelA governed semantic layer with typed dimensions, measures and hierarchies. Everything serious is built on a model.
StoryThe dashboard/report artefact. Built from responsive widgets. Can be analytic or planning-enabled.
Analytic applicationA scripted, app-like alternative to a story, built in Analytics Designer. More control, more work.
Files vs CatalogFiles is your own workspace tree. The Catalog is the curated, published shop window for consumers.
Content NetworkSAP-delivered and partner sample content and business content packages you can import into a tenant.
You must be able to
  • Explain when a dataset is enough and when the job needs a model.
  • Find your way between Files, Catalog, Connections, Modeler and Stories without hunting.
  • Describe what a tenant contains and how a story reaches a consumer.
2

Data sources, connections and modeling

The architectural core. If a scenario goes wrong, it usually went wrong here.

Connections
Live connectionSAC queries the source at runtime; no copy is made. SAP sources only. Source-system security applies. No wrangling, no model-level calculations, no planning write-back.
Import connectionA copy is acquired into SAC on a schedule. SAP and non-SAP. Full transformation, modelling and planning available.
Direct / CORS liveBrowser talks straight to the source. Fastest, keeps confidential data inside the network, supports end-to-end single sign-on. Requires CORS configuration.
Tunnel liveRouted via the Cloud Connector. Slower, but needs no CORS setup and exposes data to users outside the corporate network.
Cloud ConnectorThe secure bridge for reaching on-premise systems from the cloud.
SAC agentAdded alongside the Cloud Connector when importing from databases, universes, BW, ERP or file servers.
Modelling
AccountFinancial/measure-like members such as cost lines. One per model, mandatory in an account model, ships with a default hierarchy.
VersionSystem-generated and mandatory in planning models. Holds Actual, Budget, Planning, Forecast, Rolling Forecast. Hierarchies do not apply.
DateSystem-generated and mandatory in planning models. Defines the model's time range and granularity — year, quarter, month, week or day. Multiple Date dimensions are allowed.
OrganizationCost centres, profit centres and similar. One per model. Brings Currency and Person Responsible properties automatically, supports multiple hierarchies.
GenericEverything else. Unlimited instances per model, unlimited hierarchies. The flexible workhorse.
Data Access ControlRow-level security on the model, granting read and write per dimension member. This is how one model serves many regions.
You must be able to
  • Choose live or import from a business constraint, and say what you gave up.
  • Name what extra infrastructure a given source needs — Cloud Connector alone, or agent as well.
  • Type every dimension correctly and justify each choice.
  • Explain why a planning model cannot be built on a live connection.
Where people lose marks Answering "live, because real time is better." Real time is a requirement, not a virtue. If the same scenario also asks for cleaning, blending or planning, live is simply wrong and no amount of preference rescues it.
3

Story design and data visualization

Building the thing people actually look at.

Concepts to know cold
Responsive pageWidgets reflow into lanes so the story works on any screen, including mobile. The default choice.
Canvas pageFixed, pixel-positioned layout. Full control, no reflow. Use when print or precise composition matters.
Grid pageA spreadsheet-like sheet for tabular work and free-form calculation.
Story filterApplies across every page in the story.
Page filterApplies to one page only. Widget-level filters go narrower still.
Input controlA filter exposed to the reader so they can change it themselves.
Linked analysisSelecting in one widget filters others. Scope can be the widget, the page, or all widgets on the same model.
Conditional formattingThreshold-driven colour on measures, to encode status rather than decorate.
You must be able to
  • Pick a page type from a stated delivery requirement, mobile included.
  • Set filters at the right scope, and say why that scope and not another.
  • Choose a chart type that suits the question, not the dataset shape.
  • Wire linked analysis so a selection propagates the way the audience expects.
4

Analyzing and manipulating data in stories

What you do to the numbers once they are on screen.

Concepts to know cold
Calculated measureA new measure from a formula. Can be created at model level (import only) or at story level (both live and import).
Restricted measureAn existing measure limited to specified dimension members — revenue for one region, say.
Cross calculationA calculation placed as its own dimension in a table, so several calculations sit side by side.
BlendingLinking two models in a story on common dimensions to analyse them together. Story-level, not a permanent join.
DrillMoving through a hierarchy level by level, or across to another dimension.
VarianceBuilt-in comparison between two versions or periods, with automatic formatting.
You must be able to
  • Say which calculations survive on a live model and which do not.
  • Blend two models and name the dimensions the blend needs.
  • Build a variance between Budget and Actual and read it correctly.
  • Diagnose a slow story and name the fix — fewer widgets, tighter filters, less blending.
5

Manual planning

Where BI people start losing ground. Nothing here maps to tools you already know.

Concepts to know cold
Public versionVisible to everyone with the right privileges. A model may have only one public Actual version.
Private versionYour sandbox, tied to the story, visible only to you unless shared. Auto-saves. Becomes public when published.
Version categoriesActual, Budget, Planning, Forecast, Rolling Forecast. Categories classify what a version is for.
PublishPushes private data into a public version. Cannot be undone. "Publish As" creates a new public version instead of overwriting one.
RevertDiscards unpublished changes and returns to the last saved state.
Data entryAbsolute values, or relative ones like +10% or +5000. Copy and paste works, with aggregation on paste.
RolldownEnter at an aggregate node and SAC distributes down to leaf members automatically, keeping proportions.
Spreading & distributionManually push a total across members or periods by a chosen rule — evenly, by weight, or by an existing pattern.
Seamless planningPlanning directly on data federated from Datasphere or BW rather than a fully imported copy.
You must be able to
  • Run a Budget-versus-Actual cycle end to end without prompting.
  • Explain to a nervous planner why their private version is safe.
  • Enter a number at group level and predict exactly what happens underneath.
  • Say what publishing does and why it cannot be reversed.
Where people lose marks Treating versions as folders. A version is a dimension member that data is stored against — which is why one model can hold Actual, three Budget iterations and a Forecast at once, and why comparing them is a filter rather than a join.
6

Advanced planning features

The heaviest course in the journey, and the strongest differentiator on exam day.

The seven data action step types
CopyCopies data within one model by rules, filters and aggregation settings. One source member to many targets is allowed.
Cross-model copyMoves data between two different planning models, mapping dimension members automatically or by hand.
AllocationDistributes values across members by a driver or rule — IT costs across departments by headcount, for instance.
ConversionApplies currency conversion while copying between measures.
Fact deletionRemoves facts, strips zeros, or zeroes values out. Needs the Maintain privilege.
Embedded data actionRuns another data action inside this one, with parameters. How you build reusable workflows.
Advanced formulasScripted calculation with loops and conditions, for things like carry-forward balances, depreciation and cash flow.
The rest of area six
Multi actionBundles several data actions and import steps into one runnable, schedulable sequence. A data action works inside models; a multi action orchestrates across them.
Value driver treeA visual driver-based model. Change a driver, watch KPIs move across periods, then book the simulation back if you like it.
Data lockingFreezes a slice of data so it cannot be edited — closing a period, for example. Locks can be delegated to owners and scheduled through the Calendar.
Validation rulesDefine which dimension member combinations are legal, blocking nonsense entries such as a product sold in a region that does not stock it.
Currency conversionNeeds a currency conversion table holding rates by date and category. The Organization dimension supplies the currency property.
CalendarTasks, processes, owners and due dates for the planning cycle, plus scheduling for multi actions and locks.
You must be able to
  • Pick the right step type from a described business need, first time.
  • Say when a job needs a multi action rather than a data action.
  • Set up an allocation and name its driver.
  • Distinguish data locking from validation rules — one stops editing, the other stops nonsense.
7

Augmented analytics and collaboration

Small area. Know what each feature is for, resist the urge to go deep.

Concepts to know cold
Smart InsightsAutomatic statistical explanation of a data point or variance — what is driving this number. Works on most charts, table cells and variances.
Just AskNatural-language querying, in English, German, French, Italian or Spanish. Answers come back as a table or chart. This is the current name for what older material calls Search to Insight.
Smart PredictTrains predictive scenarios on your historical data. Three types: classification for yes/no outcomes, regression for a numeric value, time series for forecasting forward.
Smart DiscoveryAutomatically surfaces the drivers and relationships behind a target measure, as a generated story.
Analytics CompassMonte Carlo simulation for estimating outcomes under uncertainty.
JouleSAP's conversational assistant, reaching SAC data from other SAP products where enabled.
Comments vs discussionsComments attach to a data point or cell and live with the data. Discussions are conversations about the story, alongside it.
You must be able to
  • Match a business question to the right smart feature in one step.
  • Choose the correct Smart Predict scenario type from a described outcome.
  • Say where a comment lives versus where a discussion lives.

The whiteboard: where SAC sits in a BI architecture

Hofstetter built this live, bottom to top, over the first hour. It is the most useful single picture of the day because it answers the question the exam keeps asking in different costumes: which layer is responsible for this? The Modeler is the seam. Everything below it is the warehouse's job; everything above it is yours.

The one sentence to keep from this diagram

A live connection to BW hands SAC a BEx query, which is a slice of the query layer. SAC cannot use that query directly in a story; you must create one model per query. Hofstetter said it three times, and page 3 of this handbook under-sold it. The Modeler is not a convenience layer, it is the contract between the two halves.

Excursus: account-based versus key-figure-based models

Slide 6, and the clearest explanation of the two model types I have seen. The exam's model-type decision lives here. The tables are the trainer's own example; the product names are deliberately boring.

standard

Account-based model

A key figure is defined by a characteristic of the account dimension. The model holds only two physical key figures: amount and quantity. Very adaptable when the KPI portfolio is volatile, and it avoids sparse matrices, so it performs better.

measure-based

Key-figure-based model

Each key figure is its own field with its own currency or quantity unit. Obsolete key figures must stay in the model for consistency when things change, and there is a real risk of sparsely populated matrices, which costs performance. No geo functionality in this type.

And the Modeler is the starting point for every SAC tool, so a wrong model type is a wrong afternoon.

Who builds what: the trainer's three-way split

Slides 7, 8 and 10 draw a line the exam also draws: the Story Builder belongs to power users, the Analytics Designer belongs to IT, and planning is its own curriculum.

S

Story Builder — created and maintained by power users

Uses existing models. Web reports and small dashboards. Responsive design. Widgets with predefined properties, a colour palette aligned to the corporate identity, custom widgets if needed.

Analytical possibilities named on the slide
  • Adapt the model into your own analysis view inside the widget (the data view).
  • Filter globally or per widget; restricted and calculated key figures; sorting; rankings; thresholds (conditional formatting).
  • Apply a user's selection to other widgets: linked analysis.

Curriculum component: Story Builder exercise and Story Builder case study (page 7).

A

Analytics Designer — created and maintained by IT professionals

Complex web applications and dashboards on existing models. Responsive to screen width. Themes for global widget formatting, CSS for per-widget formatting, self-defined palettes for a consistent look.

  • Extra interaction widgets: buttons, drop-down menus, input fields, radio buttons.
  • Own functionality via scripts: global and local variables, start parameters passed as URL parameters, a JavaScript-like syntax with its own objects and methods.

Curriculum component: Analytics Designer case study, aimed at information-science students.

P

Authorisation: five elements and one container

Slide 13 is a graph, not a list, and the arrows are the content. Roles bundle functionalities; roles and folder shares attach to either a user or a team; users belong to teams. The workspace sits on top of all of it.

WorkspaceA container with its own folder structure, outside the regular SAC folders. Access rights are assigned at team level only; individual users cannot be assigned. Administered by workspace administrators, who do not have to be members of the authorised teams.
Why botherIt lets the project level become part of the authorisation system. Workspace users see only the workspace, never the general folder tree. Folder sharing stays inside the workspace's teams and users, and only with the Share permission.

Do them in this order, and do not skip the first one

Navigation is marked mandatory in the curriculum for a reason: the later documents assume you have set your profile, built the home screen and made your own folder. Then the Story Builder exercise (you edit a finished dashboard), then Case Study I (you build the dashboard), then Case Study II (you build the model the dashboard stands on). That reverse order, consumer before builder before modeller, is deliberate and worth copying when you teach.

Track 1 · Navigation in SAP Analytics Cloud

Case study, beginner, 22 pages. Drizi & Weidner, v3.0, March 2025. Mandatory framework module.

Track 2 · Story Builder exercise

Exercise, 17 pages, 60 minutes. You get a finished sales dashboard and three change requests from Alex Kuhn, Vice President Marketing and Sales.

The brief, in Kuhn's words

"On the Sales Quantity page, I want a filter for the calendar month in addition to the filter for the year." "On the Sales Revenue page, in the bar charts, exchange the Price per PC key figure with the Contribution Margin; remove Price per Piece from the table." "For that bar chart, choose a different colour display."

Track 3 · Case Study I: Story Builder

83 pages, 220 minutes, Hofstetter, Morelli, Thurau & Weidner. Kuhn wants a sales dashboard with five pages: Overview, Customer View (3 years), Product View, Most Popular Bikes 2019, Dynamic Analysis. The model is Global Bike (case study): a measure-based analytic model with six measures and four dimensions, revenue in both global (GC) and local (LC) currency.

36 pages, 130 minutes, Drizi, Weidner & Hofstetter. Kuhn wants the 2007–2018 sales orders stored directly in SAC, and the 2019 extract loaded on top once it exists. You design the model, load it, and test it with a simple story. This is the track that maps onto the exam's first, decisive step.

Reproducing it in your own tenant

The Global Bike workbook is UCC-licensed and lives on their tenant. The procedure is portable: any sales extract with date, product, customer and a handful of measures lets you walk Case Study II step for step in the free student edition, then Case Study I on top of it. Export a few thousand rows from any public dataset and keep the same column roles. The muscle memory is the point, not the bicycles.
